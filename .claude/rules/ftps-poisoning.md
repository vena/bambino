`FtpsClient` poisons itself after a control-channel desync. Every `write_command`/`read_response` failure, in every method — not just the `list_directory`/`upload_file`/`download_file` data-transfer window between the server's `150`/`125` reply and the matching final reply — sets `poisoned = true`. Control-channel I/O goes through `write_command_poisoning`/`read_response_poisoning` (via `command()`), and the data-channel paths poison explicitly; a new command must use those helpers, never a bare `write_command`/`read_response`, or a desync goes unrecorded (BUG-004 was seven methods missing exactly this). The only bare calls are in connect (`send_and_expect`, the greeting read), before a client exists to poison, and `disconnect`'s `QUIT`. A wrong-but-received reply code (e.g. RNFR getting something other than 350) is not a desync and does not poison — only a transport-level `write_command`/`read_response` error does. Every public method checks the flag first and returns `ProtocolViolation` once set — there is no un-poisoning, only reconnect. `delete_file`/`remove_directory` follow a `550` with a `LIST` of the parent (#392); if that listing fails at the transport level the client is poisoned and the listing's error is returned, never the `550`, which would read as a refusal on a usable session. `FtpsClient::disconnect(self)` consumes the client and returns its `(Tls, Factory, Timer)`; on a poisoned client it skips `QUIT`/`close()` and only drops the control stream. `PrinterClient::disconnect_ftps()` puts those parts back into `ftps_config`, and `ensure_ftps()` (behind `ftps()`/`connect_ftps()`) disconnects a poisoned session (`FtpsClient::is_poisoned()`) and redials, so `ftps()` never hands a poisoned client back and `is_ftps_connected()` reports `false` for one (#448).
